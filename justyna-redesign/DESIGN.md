# Design directions for review

## Physical scene

A curator and a parent browse the archive in afternoon daylight, close enough to notice paper fibres, torn edges and fragments of packaging. The interface should feel like a generous worktable, not a white cube or an online shop.

## Shared principles

- The original full-screen paper composition and handmade Cute Cut mark remain as the entrance to the site.
- Original artwork remains uncropped whenever it is the primary subject.
- Texture and irregular edges are led by the scans themselves; interface panels behave like restrained cut paper.
- Categories are always reachable and remain usable with keyboard and touch.
- Polish and English are equally complete and can be switched without losing the selected direction or category.
- Motion resembles placing, sliding and rearranging paper. No bounce or elastic easing.
- Body text stays within 68 characters per line.
- Focus states are visible and reduced-motion preferences are respected.

## Direction A, Worktable

Warm, pale ground with an asymmetric field of works. A few collages overlap the title at the opening, then settle into an open archive. Typography is sturdy, friendly and quiet.

## Direction B, Colour Pantry

A saturated categorical index derived from the background colours in the collages. Each category becomes a full-width colour room with a horizontal rail of works. The visual identity feels bold and immediately recognisable.

## Direction C, Cut-out Stage

A deep charcoal stage with one large work at a time, a compact strip of neighbouring works and a movable category index. The artwork feels luminous and theatrical without imitating a museum website.

## Interaction model

- category changes update the gallery without a page reload;
- selecting a work opens an accessible full-screen viewer;
- arrow keys move between works and Escape closes the viewer;
- optional slow drift and gentle pointer parallax stop under reduced motion;
- contact remains a normal, copyable mail link.
